## Specific points

### R1. Sect. 2.1, tool enumeration, purposes, and references

We agree and have restructured the opening of Sect. 2.1. A new paragraph now walks through the five steps of the analysis and states explicitly, for each tool, the task it performs in this work and why it follows from the previous step: HDBSCAN identifies the members as a proper-motion over-density; PyMC/NUTS then samples the parallax-distance, proper-motion, and King-profile posteriors of those members; a weighted scikit-learn kernel density estimate locates the cluster center; ASteCA, sampled with DEMetropolis because its grid-interpolated likelihood has no usable gradients, fits the age, metallicity, extinction, and distance modulus; and Sagitta characterizes the pre-main-sequence content. Every tool now carries its canonical reference and a footnote with its website at first mention (EROTICA, PyMC, HDBSCAN, scikit-learn, ASteCA, and Sagitta). The pipeline is the same code the round-1 version called COSMIC; it was renamed to avoid a collision with the `cosmic-popsynth` binary-population code, and the revised Sect. 3.1 says so at first mention.

### R2. Sect. 2.1.1, the native HDBSCAN membership probability

We agree that this quantity was not defined, and we thank the referee for insisting on it. The revised Sect. 2.1.1 now defines both factors of the product precisely: $f_i$ is the fraction of the 290 sweep runs in which source $i$ is assigned to a cluster rather than to noise, and $p_{\mathrm{HDBSCAN},i}$ is the soft cluster-membership strength that HDBSCAN derives from its condensed tree, $p_{\mathrm{HDBSCAN},i}=\lambda_i/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}$ (McInnes et al. 2017), which equals unity for sources persisting to the densest core of the proper-motion over-density, tends to zero at the cluster boundary, and is zero for noise. We also state explicitly that $\tilde{p}_i=f_i\,p_{\mathrm{HDBSCAN},i}$ is an operational ranking statistic rather than a calibrated posterior probability, and that the likely candidates are the sources of the selected NGC 6383 branch with $\tilde{p}>0.5$. While revising this passage we additionally corrected the description of $f_i$ to match the implementation exactly: it counts assignment to any cluster in a given sweep run, branch membership being enforced separately through the adopted run.

### R3. Sect. 2.1.1, the role of the density level lambda

We agree that the role of $\lambda$ was left ambiguous. The revised Sect. 2.1.1 now introduces $\lambda$ once and states its three, and only three, roles explicitly: (i) within each sweep run, the NGC 6383 candidate branch is identified as the condensed-tree branch containing the highest density level; (ii) $\lambda$ defines the per-source membership strength $p_{\mathrm{HDBSCAN},i}$ that enters the pseudo-probability (see our reply to the previous point); and (iii) $\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}$ is a stability diagnostic of the hyperparameter sweep, acting only as a tie-breaker in the choice of \texttt{min\_cluster\_size} and delimiting the stable regime displayed in Fig. B.1. The text and the appendix now state explicitly that $\lambda$ is never treated as a fitted physical parameter and that the $\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\geq8$ cut is a display criterion that affects neither the adopted configuration, which is set by maximizing the recovered branch size, nor the membership itself.

### R4. Sect. 2.1.2, notation (U, half-normal, mean parallax)

We thank the referee for flagging the undefined notation. $\mathcal{U}(a,b)$ denotes the uniform distribution on $[a,b]$, it is the prior on the mean parallax, while the half-normal prior mentioned in our previous response applies to the parallax dispersion $\sigma_\varpi$; we regret that the letter allowed these two priors to be conflated. The revised Sect. 2.1.2 now defines $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma)$, $\mathcal{U}(a,b)$, and $\mathcal{HN}(s)$ (half-normal with scale $s$) explicitly at first use, and states that this notation applies to all priors in the paper, including the King-profile priors of Sect. 2.1.5. We also now define $\bar{\varpi}$ as the arithmetic mean of the observed parallaxes of the sources entering the fit (those with fractional parallax error below 0.1), matching the implementation in the published code.

### R5. Table 1, "Distance (D.M.)"

The second line of Table 1 is the distance obtained by converting the isochrone-fit distance modulus ($10.30 \pm 0.09$ mag, third line) into a distance, $1.15 \pm 0.05$ kpc; it is not inferred from the Bailer-Jones distances. (While revising this point we also corrected the distance-modulus uncertainty, which had inadvertently duplicated the extinction uncertainty: it is $\pm0.09$ mag, and the derived distance $1.15 \pm 0.05$ kpc.) The Bailer-Jones geometric distances enter only the first line, through the hierarchical distance model of Sect. 2.1.2. We have renamed both rows ("Distance (parallax-based)" and "Distance (from distance modulus)") and extended the table caption to state the origin of each value, including the fact that the two estimates are not independent because the distance-modulus prior was centered on the parallax-based distance, as already noted in Sect. 3.2.

### R6. Sect. 2.1.3, sampler diagnostics (R-hat, ESS, divergences, E-BFMI)

We agree that these diagnostics were listed without explanation. The revised Sect. 2.1.3 now introduces each quantity in plain language: the rank-normalized $\hat{R}$ (agreement between independent chains), the bulk and tail effective sample sizes (number of effectively independent posterior draws), divergent transitions (numerical failures of the Hamiltonian integrator), and the energy Bayesian fraction of missing information, E-BFMI (efficiency of the sampler's exploration of the energy distribution). We also added the appropriate reference for the E-BFMI diagnostic (Betancourt 2016) alongside Vehtari et al. (2021), and state the recommended thresholds ($\hat{R}<1.01$, ESS $>400$, E-BFMI $>0.3$) that all our NUTS-based fits comfortably satisfy.

### R7. Sect. 2.1.5 / 2.2, prior coefficients (0.8/1.5 Tmax) and the binarity alpha/beta

The coefficients in Sect. 2.1.5 are not tuned values but weakly informative bounds anchored to the largest physically meaningful scale of the system, $T_{\rm max}=\max(R_{\rm Hill}, r_{\rm bound})=42.5'$ (Table 1): $0.8\,T_{\rm max}$ places the core-radius bound more than an order of magnitude above any realistic open-cluster core radius, while $1.5\,T_{\rm max}$ allows the outer radius to exceed the nominal bound radius, accommodating the uncertainties in the cluster mass and Galactic potential. Following the referee's suggestion we quantified the impact of the specific choice: refitting the identical profile with $0.9\,T_{\rm max}$/$1.2\,T_{\rm max}$ leaves $R_c$, $k$, $b$, and $C$ unchanged to within $0.5\sigma$ ($R_c=2.00\pm0.20$ versus the adopted $1.96^{+0.19}_{-0.16}$ arcmin) and shifts only the outer radius (median $R_t\simeq33$ versus $40^{+16}_{-17}$ arcmin, consistent within $1\sigma$), which the text already characterizes as weakly constrained and model dependent; the revised Sect. 2.1.5 now states this rationale and sensitivity test explicitly. We should be explicit that these alternative coefficients are not neutral for the fit we finally adopt: with $T_{\rm max}=42.5'$, a bound of $1.2\,T_{\rm max}=51.0'$ falls \emph{below} the adopted $R_t=54'$ of the 70 arcmin refit, so any coefficient smaller than $1.27$ would exclude the adopted value from the prior support outright rather than perturb it. This is not a second, independent limitation but the same one seen from the other side, and the revised Sect. 5 now states the arithmetic: $R_t$ is bounded from above by the prior in every window we fit, which is why we report it as conditional on that bound and quantify the relaxed-bound case (see our reply to R11). Concerning $\alpha$ and $\beta$ in Sect. 2.2: these are the two coefficients of ASteCA's parametrization of the multiplicity fraction as a function of primary mass, calibrated by ASteCA on the observed multiplicity compilation of Offner et al. (2023, Protostars & Planets VII), and they were held fixed in our fit. With $\alpha=0.09$, $\beta=0.94$ the function reproduces the observed binarity across spectral types, $\simeq0.15$ at $0.1\,M_\odot$, $\simeq0.26$ for M dwarfs, $\simeq0.5$ for solar-type stars, $\simeq0.8$ for B stars, and $\simeq0.95$ for O stars, in agreement with Duchêne & Kraus (2013) and Moe & Di Stefano (2017). The revised text now gives the calibration source and this correspondence with the observed binarity-mass relation.

### R8. Sect. 2.1.5, galactocentric distance of 7.19 kpc

The galactocentric distance follows from the cluster's Galactic coordinates and the adopted parallax-based distance ($d = 1.11 \pm 0.06$ kpc) through the law of cosines, $R_{\mathrm{gc}} = (R_\odot^{2} + d^{2} - 2 R_\odot d \cos l \cos b)^{1/2}$, adopting a solar galactocentric radius of $R_\odot = 8.3$ kpc (de Grijs & Bono 2016), which yields $7.19$ kpc; the uncertainty is propagated from the distance and cluster-center uncertainties. The revised Sect. 2.1.5 now states the formula, the adopted $R_\odot$ with its reference, and the error propagation explicitly.

### R9. Sect. 2.2, assumptions behind the MIST isochrones

We agree that ages of very young clusters are sensitive to the input physics of the adopted evolutionary models, and we now recall the key assumptions behind MIST in Sect. 2.2: we used the MIST v1.2 grids, which adopt solar-scaled abundances (Asplund et al. 2009; $Z_\odot\simeq0.0142$), a single solar-calibrated mixing length ($\alpha_{\rm MLT}=1.82$), exponentially decaying convective overshooting ($f_{\rm ov,core}=0.016$, $f_{\rm ov,env}=0.0174$), and $v/v_{\rm crit}=0.4$ with rotation initialized at the ZAMS, so the PMS tracks that dominate our fit are effectively non-rotating. We note that for a PMS-dominated fit at $\sim3.5$ Myr the leading systematic is not core overshooting, which mainly affects turn-off ages of older clusters, but the neglect of magnetic activity and starspots: magnetic and spotted PMS models (Feiden 2016; SPOTS, Somers et al. 2020) yield ages up to a factor of $\sim2$ older, so the absolute MIST PMS age scale is plausibly biased young. The revised text states these assumptions and the direction of this bias explicitly, complementing the existing statement that our quoted uncertainty is the posterior spread within the MIST family only, and the cross-comparison with alternative PMS families (PARSEC, Baraffe) remains deferred to a dedicated follow-up as noted in our previous response.

### R10. Sect. 2.2, which figure shows the nuisance parameter

The referee's inference is correct: the plot in question is the ASteCA corner plot, Fig. B.4 (Appendix B), which shows the joint and marginal distributions of the four isochrone-fit parameters together with the likelihood-scatter nuisance term $\sigma$, and whose caption now records that this DEMetropolis ensemble does not meet our convergence criteria. The sentence in Sect. 2.2 now cites the figure explicitly, and we have simplified its wording.

### R11. Sect. 3, extraction radius vs King outer radius

We have followed the referee's suggestion: the King outer radius is now measured from, rather than merely checked against, the wider extractions. At 40 arcmin, half (51%) of the R_t posterior mass lies beyond the data window, confirming that this window cannot constrain the outer structure; the 70 arcmin refit is the only one whose radial profile reaches a fitted background annulus beyond the cluster, and we now adopt its result, R_t = 54^{+7}_{-11} arcmin (17.4^{+2.3}_{-3.6} pc), in the abstract, Table 1, Sect. 5 (Cluster structure), and the conclusions, consistent within 1 sigma with all narrower windows, but properly background-constrained. The core radius is retained from the 40 arcmin reference sample because the 60-70 arcmin windows demonstrably admit additional field contamination (114 of the 376 sources added at 70 arcmin lie inside the 40 arcmin footprint, and the R.A. proper-motion dispersion nearly doubles), which artificially lowers R_c; the selection-stable 50 arcmin run confirms R_c = 1.90^{+0.15}_{-0.13} arcmin against the adopted 1.96^{+0.19}_{-0.16}. The paragraphs justifying the 40 arcmin choice have been shortened accordingly, Table D.2 now reports medians with 16-84% intervals, and a figure of the 70 arcmin profile fit has been added to Appendix D. We additionally note, and now state explicitly in Sect. 5 and Appendix D, that all R_t posteriors are truncated from above by the physically motivated prior bound R_t < 1.5 T_max = 63.7 arcmin: relaxing the bound to the extraction edge (R_t < 70 arcmin) shifts the adopted value from 54(+7/-11) to 58(+9/-13) arcmin, consistent within 1 sigma, with the upper tail still prior-limited. The quoted R_t is therefore presented as conditional on this prior throughout, including in the abstract and the conclusions, and the prior-sensitivity discussion of Sect. 5 (see our reply to the priors point above) now covers the adopted fit as well. A comparison of the adopted R_t with the smaller literature tidal radii (15-30 arcmin), and the reason for the difference (window and background treatment), has been added to Sect. 8.4.

### R12. Sect. 3.4, the incomprehensible KS sentence

We agree that this sentence was too compressed and have rewritten it in full. The revised text states explicitly what is compared: the radial distribution of the faintest magnitude quartile (G = 17.93-20.66 mag) is tested against each of the three brighter quartiles, giving p = 0.010 and 0.052 against the two intermediate quartiles and p = 0.42 against the brightest one, with only the first difference surviving a Holm correction over the three tests. We now also spell out the logic of the interpretation: if the reduced central concentration of the faintest stars were of dynamical origin, the strongest contrast would be expected against the brightest (most massive) quartile, whereas we find the two to be statistically indistinguishable; the pattern instead matches Gaia incompleteness at G >~ 18 mag in the crowded cluster center, consistent with the completeness limit at ~1 kpc cited in the same paragraph.

### R13. Sect. 3.4, how secondary masses enter the system mass

The secondary masses are not derived from an assumed universal mass ratio; they are per-star estimates provided by ASteCA's synthetic-population analysis, and we have added an explanation of the mechanism to Sect. 3.4. For each of the 200 synthetic-cluster realizations drawn from the posterior of the fitted parameters, each observed star is matched to the photometrically closest synthetic star; the medians over the realizations give the per-star primary and secondary masses, and the fraction of realizations in which the match is a binary gives the binary probability. The mass ratios of the synthetic binaries follow the Duchêne & Kraus (2013) distribution already specified in Sect. 2.2, to which the new text now explicitly refers. For the 51 stars whose secondary is added to the system mass (binary probability > 0.7), the resulting effective mass ratios span q = 0.26-0.73 with a median of 0.60; this is now stated in the text.

### R14. Sect. 3.4, segregation time, HD 159176, and the 99.2% subsample

We agree on both points. First, we now state explicitly that t_seg scales inversely with the mass of the most massive member: if HD 159176 (O7V + O7V; component masses of ~44-46 Msun, Penny et al. 2016) were a cluster member, the minimum segregation time would drop from 2.38 Myr to ~0.7 Myr (~0.4 Myr if the binary is treated as a single ~90 Msun dynamical unit), a factor of 3-7 shorter, which would only strengthen the conclusion that the most massive object has had time to segregate dynamically; the limiting mass of the low-mass subsample discussed below would shift only from 9.1 to ~10.3 Msun, leaving that analysis unchanged. Second, the passage the referee found incomprehensible arose from using t_seg both as a single number (for the most massive star) and, implicitly, as a per-star function of mass. We have rewritten it: Eq. (7) now defines the mass-dependent timescale t_seg(m) = (<m>/m) t_rh, and setting t_seg(m) equal to the 3.53 Myr cluster age yields a limiting mass of 9.1 Msun; stars below this limit - 252 of the 254 reference stars (99.2%), spanning 0.19-8.49 Msun, with only the two most massive members (13.13 and 13.56 Msun) excluded - cannot yet have segregated through two-body relaxation, so any segregation within this subsample must be primordial or early-dynamical. The caption of the corresponding appendix figure has been aligned with this wording.

### R17. Sect. 3.5.4, added value with respect to published catalogs

We have added a closing paragraph to Sect. 3.5.4 stating explicitly what the present study adds relative to the published catalogs. In brief, those catalogs are homogeneous all-sky compilations in which each cluster is processed with a single fixed pipeline configuration, whereas for NGC 6383 we provide sampled posterior distributions, with reported uncertainties and convergence diagnostics, for the astrometric and King structural parameters and for the parallax-based distance; for the isochrone fit we report the ensemble together with the explicit statement that it does not meet our convergence criteria (Sect. 2.1.3, Appendix B), and adopt a systematic age range in place of a posterior width, which is itself information a fixed-pipeline catalog does not supply. We also provide a per-star CDS catalog with membership pseudo-probabilities, masses, binary probabilities, and PMS quantities, and cluster-specific analyses (PMS census with an H-alpha cross-match, mass segregation and dynamical state, individual treatment of HD 159176) that survey pipelines do not attempt. Regarding the concern that code-parameter choices might impact the member selection and the derived parameters: we agree, and this is precisely why the manuscript quantifies that dependence rather than adopting one configuration, the HDBSCAN hyperparameter sweep is marginalized into the per-star pseudo-probabilities (Appendix B), the search-window dependence is measured directly with the 50-70 arcmin re-runs (Appendix D), and the mass-segregation signal is checked for stability against the classification threshold (Sect. 3.4); this per-cluster sensitivity budget is not available in the compared catalogs. Where a quantity is found to be configuration-sensitive (the outer membership and the King outer radius), we say so explicitly and label it as window- or model-dependent. We have deliberately framed the comparison as complementarity (breadth versus per-cluster depth with a quantified error budget) rather than superiority.
