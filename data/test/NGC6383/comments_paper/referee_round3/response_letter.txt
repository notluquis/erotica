# Response to the referee, round 3

We thank the referee for the positive assessment of the revised manuscript and for the two remaining suggestions, both of which we have adopted. This round involves no restructuring, so all section, table, and figure numbers are unchanged from the previous version. The changes are highlighted in the marked-change PDF.

## Point 1. The R_V value behind A_V (Table 1, Fig. B.4; Sect. 6.1)

The isochrone fit adopted a fixed total-to-selective extinction ratio R_V = 3.1, with the Cardelli, Clayton & Mathis (1989) extinction law and the optical coefficients of O'Donnell (1994), as implemented in ASteCA. The previous version did not state this. A normal extinction law is also supported within the cluster itself: van den Ancker et al. (2000, their Sect. 4) found no deviation from R_V = 3.1 in any of the 13 stars of the cluster core for which they determined it, and Sect. 6.1 now cites this. Sect. 6.1 now gives R_V and the extinction law; the footnote of Table 1 refers to it, and the caption of Fig. B.4 states the adopted R_V.

To make the comparison with the literature color excesses of Table A.1 direct, Sect. 6.1 also gives the corresponding color excess, E(B-V) = A_V/R_V = 0.40 +/- 0.08 mag, where the +/- inherits the ensemble spread of A_V. It lies within the 0.26-0.51 mag range of the literature determinations compiled in Table A.1: above most of those published up to 2019 (0.26-0.35 mag), consistent with the 0.39-0.47 mag of the three most recent (He et al. 2022; Hunt & Reffert 2024; Pulgar-Escobar & Henriquez-Salgado 2024), and below the 0.51 +/- 0.03 mag of Aidelman et al. (2018).

## Point 2. The theoretical PMS models behind the Sagitta ages (Sect. 6.2)

No theoretical PMS evolutionary model enters Sagitta directly: its age regressor is trained on empirical labels (McBride et al. 2021). Because this answers the question only one level down, we traced the labels to the models underneath them, and Sect. 6.2 now summarizes that chain. In full:

1. Most labels are the mean ages of the Gaia moving groups of Kounkel et al. (2020), inferred with their Auriga neural network.

2. Auriga was trained on real-cluster photometry, augmented with copies of that photometry shifted in distance and reddening rather than with synthetic isochrones. The ages of those clusters come from:
   - the catalog of Kounkel & Covey (2019), whose ages come from BASE-9 fits with the PARSEC isochrones (Marigo et al. 2017), which in that work cannot return ages below log(age/yr) = 7.4, and otherwise from a network trained on MWSC cluster ages and on synthetic clusters built from PARSEC;
   - Bossini et al. (2019), with PARSEC v1.2S, for clusters older than 10 Myr only;
   - Liu & Pang (2019), with the Padova/PARSEC isochrones of Marigo et al. (2017), with clusters younger than 10 Myr excluded from the Auriga training set;
   - the MWSC catalog (Kharchenko et al. 2012, 2013), with the Padova isochrones of Marigo et al. (2008) for the main sequence, Pisa models for late-K and M stars, and the pre-main-sequence models of Siess et al. (2000).
   Below about 10 Myr, the model layers that can be identified in Auriga's training ages are therefore PARSEC and Siess et al. (2000).

3. Because Auriga favors the oldest stars in a region and so overestimates the ages of its younger members (McBride et al. 2021), well-studied star-forming regions were relabeled with literature ages: 31 of the 32 regions in Table 1 of McBride et al. (2021) take their ages from the Handbook of Star Forming Regions (2008), together with individual-star ages from 14 literature catalogs and, for Orion, the group ages of Kounkel et al. (2018), themselves based on PARSEC-COLIBRI isochrones with empirical offsets. The Handbook ages rest on different models from region to region; for NGC 2264, for example, the chapter by Dahm (2008) quotes, from a single study, median ages from 0.9 to 4.3 Myr depending on the PMS tracks adopted.

The Sagitta age scale below about 10 Myr is therefore a literature-anchored compilation that cannot be reduced to a single set of theoretical models, and it is not the MIST v1.2 scale of our isochrone fit. It has, however, been calibrated empirically against several grids: in the Orion Complex (a region that is itself part of the Sagitta training set), Kounkel et al. (2023, their Appendix A) find Sagitta ages 0.14 dex older than MIST, 0.17 dex older than PARSEC, and closest to Baraffe et al. (2015) (-0.05 dex) and to the SPOTS models with 17% spot coverage (+0.01 dex). Sect. 6.2 now gives these offsets. Only the Marigo et al. (2017), Kharchenko et al. (2013), Siess et al. (2000), Baraffe et al. (2015), SPOTS and Kounkel et al. (2023) links are cited in the manuscript, to keep the paragraph proportionate; the remaining sources above are listed here for completeness.

Tracing this provenance showed four things that bear on how the manuscript used Sagitta, and we have corrected the text for all four:

(a) NGC 6383 is itself one of the regions relabeled in the Sagitta training set, with an age of 2 Myr after Rauw & De Becker (2008) (McBride et al. 2021, their Table 1). That chapter reviews the 1.7 +/- 0.4 Myr derived by FitzGerald et al. (1978) for eight pre-main-sequence stars in the cluster core with the pre-main-sequence isochrones of Iben & Talbot (1966); the chapter's abstract describes the cluster as very young (about 2 Myr) without attaching that value to a study, and our reading is that it corresponds to the FitzGerald et al. value. Sect. 6.2 now states the training label and that the median Sagitta age of our PMS candidates, log(age/yr) = 6.75, lies 0.45 dex above it, so these ages do not simply reproduce the label.

(b) Sect. 6.1 described the median Sagitta age, 0.2 dex above the isochrone mode, as one of the "independent indicators" of an older age, and Sect. 3.1 said that the adopted age interval is set by "the spread among independent age indicators". The 0.2 dex offset is comparable to the 0.14 dex difference between the Sagitta and MIST age scales that Kounkel et al. (2023) measured in Orion, and the isochrones that enclose the bulk of the PMS locus come from the same model grid and data as the fit. Sect. 6.1 now states both and no longer treats either as independent evidence, and Sect. 3.1 now says that the interval is set by the pre-main-sequence model systematics and by the judgment described in point (g). Sect. 6.2 also states that the characteristic Sagitta age uncertainty, about 0.3 dex, comes from the dispersion of the ages assigned to the same stars placed at different distances (McBride et al. 2021).

(c) Sect. 6.2 stated, citing Kounkel et al. (2023), that Sagitta offers stable ages across G, K, and M-type stars within a population, surpassing isochrone techniques. On reading Kounkel et al. (2023), its coevality test covers K- and M-type members of Orion subgroups, not G-type stars, and its authors note that the result is partly by construction; the G/K/M phrasing appears to come from a later paper that paraphrases it. Sect. 6.2 now states what Kounkel et al. (2023) show, with that scope.

(d) Sect. 6.1 justified the asymmetric age interval by stating that no effect identified here or in the literature pushes the age of this cluster younger. A statement about the whole literature is not one we can support, and the sentence now says only what it can: that the model-family effects identified push the age older, and that we have not identified an effect that would shift the age of the cluster as a whole younger.

(e) While revising the footnote of Table 1 we found that it says "the remaining three" of five ensemble-based rows after setting the age apart; the remaining rows are four, and the footnote now says so.

(f) Sect. 6.1 attributed to Feiden (2016) an age shift of about a factor of two "at 3 Myr". That factor refers to the K- and M-type stars of the Upper Scorpius association, at about 10 Myr; the sentence now says so. The 0.3-1.0 dex range quoted there, and so the upper bar of the age interval, is unchanged.

(g) The lower bar of the age interval. While checking the reasoning behind the adopted interval, log(age/yr) = 6.55 (+0.40/-0.20), we found that the manuscript justified its upper bar and its asymmetry but never stated where the -0.20 dex came from. It is a judgment, not a measurement, and Sect. 6.1 now says so: no effect we identified shifts the age of the cluster as a whole younger, so the lower bar only moderately widens the 0.15 dex marginal standard deviation, itself a lower bound; and the young side of the PMS locus cannot set it, because unresolved binaries, differential reddening, and accretion variability broaden the locus. In checking that last point we also found that Sect. 6.1 overstated how well the isochrones of log(age/yr) = 6.20 and 6.80 bracket the PMS locus: about 60% of the PMS candidates lie between them, with the rest on either side. The text now says that these isochrones enclose the bulk of the locus and gives that fraction, and Table 1, the abstract, and the conclusions describe the corresponding 1.6-6.3 Myr range in the same terms. The interval and all values that depend on it are unchanged.

(h) Table A.1 color excesses. The three most recent studies publish the extinction (A_0 or A_V), not E(B-V), so the E(B-V) listed for them was already a conversion. Rechecking against the catalogs, the value for Hunt & Reffert (2024) was converted incorrectly: their A_V = 1.21 mag gives 0.39, not 0.38. The table now lists 0.39, and its footnote states which rows are A_V/R_V with R_V = 3.1.

(i) Dependence of the mass segregation on the outer candidates. While checking a recent study of NGC 6383 (Zhang et al. 2026), which reports no significant segregation of the five most massive stars on the high-probability Hunt & Reffert (2024) members, 90% of them within 6 arcmin of the center, we examined how our result depends on the sample. The ten most massive members lie at a median radius of about 4 arcmin in every sample, so the contrast is set by how far the low-mass population extends. The 76 reference stars absent from the Hunt & Reffert (2024) membership lie at a median radius of 28.7 arcmin, have masses below 2.8 M_sun, and 67 of them are classified as single. Restricting the sample to the 178 stars in common, or to r < 15 arcmin, keeps Lambda_MSR > 1 at N_MST = 5, 10, 15, and 20 but lowers it to 1.1-2.0 (local p = 0.02-0.35), and the single-versus-binary KS p-value rises from 0.010 to about 0.8. A sample without the extended low-mass population cannot show the contrast, so this does not argue against segregation; but the signal does require the outer candidates to be members, which is not established. The flat background term of the King fit, b = 0.011 +/- 0.007 stars/arcmin^2, corresponds to about 55 of the 254 stars, but it is poorly constrained at 40 arcmin and can absorb a corona as well as contamination; the fraction of Sagitta PMS candidates falls from 52% inside 10 arcmin to 27% at 20-40 arcmin. Removing the background implied by b at random, annulus by annulus (60 realizations, never removing the 20 most massive members), gives median local p-values for Lambda_MSR of 0.001-0.03, 0.01-0.06, and 0.06-0.24, and for the KS test of 0.03, 0.13, and 0.46, for b = 0.004, 0.011, and 0.018, respectively; Lambda_MSR stays above unity in all cases. Sect. 7 now reports all of this and describes the segregation as conditional on the membership of the outer candidates; the abstract and the conclusions carry the same qualification. The interpretation of the segregation, primordial or early dynamical, is unchanged. The numbers come from two scripts added to the analysis repository.

The only tabulated value that changes is the E(B-V) of Hunt & Reffert (2024) in Table A.1, per point (h). The mass-segregation result is now stated as conditional, per point (i); no other conclusion changes. Nine figures were regenerated only to correct the text inside them (keys and axis labels), with the data unchanged: in Fig. 6 the key now reads "Ensemble draws" rather than "Posterior draws", matching Sect. 3.1; the axis labels of Fig. B.4 use the symbols of its caption; the keys of Figs. 2 and 3 no longer use a mean symbol for the adopted distance mode or C for the proper-motion center; and the keys and axis labels of Figs. 9, B.1, C.3, C.4, and C.5 are in sentence case. References added: Cardelli et al. (1989), O'Donnell (1994), Marigo et al. (2017), Kharchenko et al. (2013), Baraffe et al. (2015), van den Ancker et al. (2000), and Zhang et al. (2026).
